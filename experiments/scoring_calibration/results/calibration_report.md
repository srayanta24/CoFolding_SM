## 1. Calibration — what does a real binder score?

Basis: 32 leak-free `dev` complexes, confirmed binders by construction (every SAbDab entry is a crystallised antibody-antigen complex).

- Median real binder: **ipTM 0.699**
- A 0.6 cutoff rejects **28%** of real binders
- A 0.8 cutoff rejects **78%** of real binders

The 0.8 mark this project used before step 1 would have discarded most genuine antibodies. 0.6 (imported from Bennett et al.'s AlphaFold3 results) transfers to this Boltz-2 pipeline reasonably well, but is not conservative — it still rejects about a quarter of real binders.

## 2. Memorisation gap

| | n | median ipTM |

| clean (post-cutoff, leak-free) | 32 | 0.699 |
| contaminated (train-era) | 100 | 0.787 |

Gap **+0.088**; P(contaminated > clean) = **0.628** (0.5 would be no difference). A real but modest effect.

Note the confound runs *against* this being an overestimate: the contaminated set skews high-affinity, and section 3 shows high-affinity complexes score *lower*, so affinity differences would shrink this gap, not inflate it.

## 3. Dose-response — ipTM does not track affinity

| Kd band | n | median ipTM |
|---|---|---|
| pM (<100pM) | 17 | 0.710 |
| 100pM-10nM | 52 | 0.775 |
| 10nM-1uM | 27 | 0.818 |
| weak (>1uM) | 4 | 0.824 |

Spearman ipTM vs log10(Kd) = **+0.134** over 100 complexes spanning nine orders of magnitude. Expected sign is negative (tighter binders should score higher).

**This is the pilot's most consequential result.** ipTM is the primary ranking signal in BoltzGen's own selection (weight 1 in Algorithm 2) and the metric this project's funnel is gated on, yet across real complexes with measured affinities it carries no information about binding strength — and the trend is mildly *backwards*. It plausibly measures whether a credible interface can be modelled at all, which is a different and more nearly binary quantity than affinity.

Caveats: the weakest band is thin, and larger/harder complexes may be both lower-affinity in this corpus and harder to model, which a size-controlled analysis would need to separate.

## 4. Separation — weak, but not absent

matched_positive vs hard_negative (both AbDesign-DB curated).

| | n | median ipTM |
|---|---|---|
| matched_positive (binding retained) | 100 | 0.790 |
| hard_negative (binding destroyed) | 100 | 0.695 |

**ROC-AUC = 0.634.** Restricted to the 17 parent complexes holding both a retained and a destroyed mutant — which controls for complex identity — positives score higher in **9/17**.

Consistent with section 3 rather than contradicting it: ipTM weakly detects a mutation that *abolishes* binding while being blind to where on the affinity scale a complex sits. For reference, the autoresearch filter paper's untuned baseline was AUC 0.637 and its tuned rule reached 0.806, so raw ipTM here sits at roughly their starting point.
