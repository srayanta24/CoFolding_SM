# Experiment 03 — Can anything rank antibodies by affinity against a fixed antigen?

Status: **complete** · run 2026-10-19/24 · 330 complexes, ~35 h GPU + ~1 h CPU

Third in the scoring-calibration series, after
[EXPERIMENT_01](EXPERIMENT_01_iptm_calibration.md) (calibration) and
[EXPERIMENT_02](EXPERIMENT_02_alternative_metrics.md) (alternative metrics). Numbers
are regenerated by `analyze_asd.py` → [results/asd_comparison.md](results/asd_comparison.md).

> **This document corrects a claim I reported on 2026-10-20** and which appears in
> commit `c5751a1`'s surrounding work: that ipTM shows no within-antigen affinity
> signal (pooled ρ = −0.038). That number came from a benchmark where two of three
> targets had **inverted ground truth** (§4). On a label-validated benchmark ipTM
> reaches pooled ρ = **+0.194**. The original claim was wrong and is withdrawn — though
> §6 explains why the corrected number is also not a clean positive.

---

## 1. What was the experiment

EXPERIMENT_02 could not test affinity ranking at all. On that benchmark 78% of
log10(Kd) variance sat *between* antigens, so an antigen-only null control scored
+0.341 — stronger than any real metric — and the whole cross-complex column had to be
discarded as confounded. The conclusion was explicitly "untested, not disproven."

This experiment builds the benchmark that removes the confound by construction: **one
antigen per target group, many antibodies, only the heavy chain varying.** Within a
target, antigen identity is constant, so it cannot explain anything.

## 2. What were we testing

**H1 — within-antigen ranking.** Does any metric we can compute rank antibodies
against a fixed antigen by their measured affinity? This is close to the question a
design campaign poses (rank many candidates against one target), and EXPERIMENT_02
established it is the only comparison worth measuring.

**H2 — is the backbone the bottleneck?** AbBiBench reports ESM-IF1 ≈0.28 and
ProteinMPNN ≈0.30 **on this exact data**, scored against crystal structures.
EXPERIMENT_02 got −0.037 from ESM-IF1 on our predicted structures and attributed the
gap to backbone quality. That was a hypothesis; this tests it against the published
number.

**H3 (null control).** `ll_antigen` — ESM-IF1's score for the antigen chain, which is
byte-identical across every entry within a target and therefore *cannot* carry affinity
information. Carried because the same control invalidated two of three columns in
EXPERIMENT_02.

## 3. What was the result

| target | n | ipTM | pTM | pLDDT | ll_heavy | ll_antibody | **ll_antigen [null]** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1n8z (HER2) | 110 | **+0.208** | +0.211 | −0.291 | −0.067 | +0.013 | **−0.056** |
| 3gbn (flu HA) | 110 | +0.273 | +0.349 | **+0.554** | +0.194 | +0.141 | **+0.284** |
| 4fqi (flu HA) | 110 | −0.009 | +0.000 | −0.117 | +0.114 | +0.132 | +0.079 |
| **pooled** | **330** | **+0.194** | +0.204 | +0.060 | +0.078 | +0.083 | **+0.105** |

**H1 — partially, and only on one target cleanly.** ipTM reaches +0.194 pooled, which
is in the same range as AbBiBench's published baselines. But see §6: on 3gbn the null
control scores +0.284, so that target's numbers cannot be read at face value.

**H2 — the backbone hypothesis survives but is not cleanly measured.** ESM-IF1 reaches
+0.078 pooled against a published ≈0.28 on the same data with crystal structures. The
direction is consistent with predicted backbones degrading it. But with the null at
+0.105 — *higher than ESM-IF1* — the size of the degradation cannot be stated.

**H3 — the control fired, for the third experiment running.**

## 4. What dataset did we use, and the failure that produced it

`databases/asd/`'s `dataset == "abbd"` subset **is AbBiBench** — 155,853 rows matching
its published count, with `source_url` pointing at the HuggingFace dataset. It had been
sitting unused in this repo since August. Built by `build_asd_sets.py` into
`sets/asd_manifest.tsv`.

**Final set**: 330 entries, 110 each on 4fqi (flu HA), 3gbn (flu HA), 1n8z (HER2); mean
886 residues; −log KD spreads 2.72–3.41; all ten affinity deciles covered per target.

### The first build was invalid, and the failure is the useful part

**ASD labels every `abbd` row `affinity_type == "-log KD"`, and that label is wrong for
some targets.** Checked across the full subset:

| target | range | % negative | plausible as −log KD? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4fqi, 3gbn, 1n8z, 1mhp, aayl* | 5.60 … 12.43 | 0% | yes |
| **2fjg** | −3.01 … 0.50 | **46%** | **no** |
| **1mlc** | −4.10 … 10.18 | 8% | **no** |

A −log KD of −3 implies Kd = 10³ M, which is not a binding constant. Those columns are
almost certainly a ΔΔG-like or log-fold-change quantity centred on the wild type, where
**the sign convention is inverted**.

**Why it hit us**: the first build sorted candidates cheapest-first and took three, and
the three cheapest targets are exactly the mislabelled ones. *Selecting on cost without
validating the ground-truth label* produced a benchmark pointing the wrong way on two of
three targets.

It would have been easy to publish. The v1 ESM-IF1 result came out −0.286 (1mhp),
−0.253 (1mlc), **+0.462** (2fjg) — a dramatic sign split that reads as a genuine
target-dependent effect, with one target *beating* AbBiBench's published number. It was
an artefact. The v1 tables are retained as `results/asd_*_v1_mislabelled.tsv`.

Fixes now in `build_asd_sets.py`: an **affinity-plausibility gate** rejecting any target
outside a physically possible −log KD range; selection by **most antibodies** rather than
cheapest; and **replicate aggregation before stratifying** (4fqi has 130,629 rows over
65,536 distinct heavies, and deduplicating *after* stratification had silently shrunk a
requested 110 to 62).

## 5. How did we test the hypothesis

Each entry cofolded through the identical path as EXPERIMENT_01/02 — Boltz-2, no MSA,
heavy + light + antigen as three chains (`score_sets.py`, now taking `--manifest` and
`--out` so one scorer serves both benchmarks without the results mixing). ESM-IF1 scored
each chain conditioned on the whole complex (`compute_esmif.py`). Spearman throughout;
pooling z-scores within each target first, so a target with a different absolute metric
scale cannot dominate.

Cost: **34.8 h GPU** for 330 cofolds at 886 residues mean (6.3 min each — complex size
drove this well above EXPERIMENT_01's 3.1 min at 600 residues), plus 57 min CPU for
ESM-IF1.

**Leakage is acknowledged, not controlled.** These are natural antibody variants against
well-studied antigens with deposited structures, almost certainly inside Boltz-2's
training window. This measures *ranking ability under favourable conditions*, not
generalisation. A weak result here is therefore more damning than a strong one is
encouraging.

## 6. What were the findings

**1. The withdrawn claim.** ipTM does carry within-antigen affinity signal — pooled
+0.194, and +0.208 on 1n8z against a null of −0.056. My 2026-10-20 report of ρ ≈ 0 was
produced on inverted ground truth and is wrong.

**2. But the null control fires again, so most of the signal is not interpretable.**
On 3gbn the antigen-only control reaches **+0.284** — as strong as ipTM's +0.273 on the
same target, and stronger than every ESM-IF1 column. Pooled, the null (+0.105) **beats
ESM-IF1** (+0.078). The antigen is constant within a target, so this is target-specific
leakage: plausibly, overall prediction quality covaries with affinity within 3gbn, and
every metric that reads the structure inherits it.

**What actually survives is one cell**: ipTM on 1n8z, +0.208 against a null of −0.056.
One target, n=110, uncorrected for the eight metrics × three targets examined.

**3. ESM-IF1 does not reproduce its published number here** (+0.078 vs ≈0.28). The
backbone-quality hypothesis from EXPERIMENT_02 survives as the leading explanation, but
the null floor means the gap cannot be quantified.

**4. The "variants too similar to resolve" hypothesis is refuted.** 4fqi is a genuine
null (−0.009) while 3gbn, the same antigen class, appears strong. If diversity explained
it, 4fqi should be the least diverse. It is not: 1n8z has 21 variable positions, 4fqi
16, 3gbn **11** — the target with the *fewest* variable positions shows the *strongest*
apparent signal. That hypothesis should not be carried forward.

**5. Three experiments, three times the null control changed the conclusion.** It cost
one extra function call each time. Without it this document would have reported
"ipTM achieves ρ = +0.27 on 3gbn, comparable to published inverse-folding baselines."

### Where this leaves the programme

**We still do not have a demonstrated selection signal.** Across three benchmarks the
positive results are: packstat 14/17 (EXPERIMENT_02, p=0.006 uncorrected, 17 parent
complexes) and ipTM +0.208 on one target here. Each is a single thin cell that would not
survive correction for the number of comparisons made.

That is the honest state, and it directly gates the agent question
(IMPROVE_DESIGN.md §5): a selection agent cannot be built on this, while an
analyst/executor agent does not depend on it.

### Limitations

- **The null floor is the dominant limitation.** Until the source of target-specific
  leakage is identified, no per-target correlation on this benchmark can be read at face
  value. Isolating it is the obvious next step and needs no new GPU time — the candidate
  is prediction quality covarying with affinity, testable from data already on disk.
- Three targets, two of them the same antigen class (influenza HA). 1n8z (HER2) is the
  only independent one, and it is the only clean cell.
- No multiple-comparison correction has been applied anywhere in this document.
- Leakage is uncontrolled by design (see §5).
- 4fqi holds 65,536 distinct heavy chains and only 110 were sampled; if the leakage
  question resolves, deeper sampling on a single target is cheap in design effort though
  not in GPU.
